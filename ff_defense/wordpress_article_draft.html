Streaming a defense every week is one of those lineup decisions that never really gets easier — one week you're chasing a plus matchup against a rookie QB, the next you're trying to figure out if a top-10 unit on paper is actually worth trusting on the road. The Fantasy Football Defense Stream-O-Matic takes the guesswork out of it by pulling together the matchup factors that actually matter — Vegas game scripts, pressure rates, offensive and defensive efficiency, expert consensus, and more — into one weekly-updated chart, so you can see at a glance which defenses are set up to produce and which ones you should leave on your bench. Below is the key explaining exactly how the chart is built and scored, including links to every source behind it, if you want to dig into the "why" behind any team's ranking.

How the Chart Works

📊 Click to Show/Hide: Scoring & Categories Key

Scoring

Point totals are assigned to each team using a traditional rotisserie scoring style commonly used in fantasy baseball season-long leagues. Every scored category is ranked 1 through 32, and a team's SCORE is the sum of those ranks. In every column, a higher number is better for that defense — whether the category measures the defense itself or the offense it's about to face. The team with the highest total sits at the top of the chart.

Categories

The categories for the chart are listed below, along with links to websites I use to find this data.
  • Rost%/Start%: Roster percentages based on Yahoo Fantasy Football leagues. This gives us an idea of who is available. This doesn't factor into the standings.
  • Location (Home): Defenses generally play better at home than on the road. All home teams receive a five-point bonus. Road teams show an "@" in front of their opponent.
  • Vegas (Opp Implied): Using Vegas odds, pulled from odds-api. The spread and over/under generate an implied points total for each team. This game score portrays what the game flow could look like and, therefore, which fantasy football defenses are in a good position to succeed. The defense facing the lowest implied total gets the most points.
  • Def Rating: My measure of how good a defense actually is, built from nflverse's public play-by-play data. It combines two things: how much expected point value a defense gives up per play, and how often it stops the offense from gaining what it needed on a given down. Early in the year, when nobody has enough snaps to judge, it leans on my preseason defensive rankings and shifts toward real 2026 performance as the sample grows — so Week 2 doesn't overreact to one blowout. The best defense gets 32 points.
  • Opp Off EPA: How good the offense across from them is, measured in Expected Points Added per play. EPA asks a simple question on every snap: given down, distance and field position, how many points was this offense expected to score before the play — and after it? A 7-yard gain on 3rd-and-5 adds expected points; a 7-yard gain on 3rd-and-12 subtracts them. Averaged out over a season, it's one of the cleanest ways to separate offenses that move the ball from offenses that pile up empty yards. Also calculated from nflverse play-by-play. The weaker the offense a defense is facing, the more points it gets here.
  • Pressure (Opp Prss%): Thanks to Sharp Football, we have access to advanced passing statistics. I focused on pressure percentages allowed here, which includes QB hurries, hits, and sacks. The defense facing the offensive line that allows the most pressure gets the most points.
  • ECR: The team that is ranked #1 on FantasyPros ECR for the week gets the maximum points for the week.
  • Proj: Yahoo! projected points for the week, plus Subvertadown's situational adjustment. Subvertadown's model accounts for the week-specific stuff a straight projection tends to flatten out, and he shares those adjustments with me directly — so this column is Yahoo's baseline with his read layered on top, as one number. It's scored the same roto way as everything else: the highest projection for the week gets 32 points, the lowest gets 1. Ranking it keeps one column of fantasy points from swamping five columns of 1-32 ranks.
