Purpose of the Fantasy Football Auction Draft Tool

Before I get into the specifics, I want to be clear about the goal here. This tool is not telling you who to draft or when to stop bidding. That decision is yours. Maybe you want to go stars-and-scrubs and load up at the top of your budget. Maybe you'd rather spread your money evenly across a deeper roster. Both are valid approaches, and the tool works the same way either way. It just tells you what things actually cost as your specific draft unfolds. The values are built from real season-long projections, blended with my own personal rankings as a secondary signal, then converted into dollar values using the same market-calibrated formula across every team count and scoring format we support. Projections update regularly, so make sure you're working from a current copy before your draft. Here's the part that makes an auction tool actually useful, versus just another static values sheet: once your draft starts, you enter the real price a player sold for, and the tool re-rates everyone else in that same tier of value based on it. If quarterbacks are going for well over their starting value early, the tool will show you that trend before you're the one stuck overpaying for the last one on the board. If a tier is going for less than expected, that shows up too, and maybe that's exactly where you look to get a discount.

How to Use the Auction Draft Tool

Setup

Start on the Instructions tab. It walks through the same setup and column explanations covered here, right inside the workbook. From there, head to the Setup tab. You'll enter:
  • Starting Budget ($): your league's per-team auction budget.
  • Number of Teams: a dropdown of the team counts we've calibrated real data for.
  • Scoring Format: Standard, Half-PPR, or Full PPR.
  • Roster Shape: starters at QB/RB/WR/TE, plus independent counts for each flex type (RB/WR/TE flex, RB/WR flex, WR/TE flex, and Superflex; these can combine, so a league with one standard flex and one superflex slot is no problem), DEF, K, and bench size.

Draft Board Preparation

Each position (QB, RB, WR, TE) has its own color-coded block of columns: Player, Team, Proj Pts, Starting Value, Price Paid, and Market Value. Every position's columns are sortable and filterable on their own, independent of the other positions. Click the dropdown arrow on any position's header row to sort or filter that block. This matters most right after you change something on Setup: Proj Pts updates live when you switch Scoring Format, for example, but row order only gets set once, so re-sort a block any time you've changed settings and want it back in the right order. Draft Board Use

Starting Value is what we'd expect a player to cost before your draft has actually started: your pre-draft baseline. Market Value is the live number: it starts out equal to Starting Value, and moves as real prices come in during your draft. As players get drafted, by you or anyone else in your league, enter the actual winning bid in that player's Price Paid column. You don't need to delete rows or resort anything for this part; every remaining player's Market Value updates on its own. A single pick won't swing anyone's price much. The tool waits for a real pattern, a couple of prices landing near each other in value, before it re-rates a tier, and even then it's a modest, capped adjustment, not a dramatic swing. Separately, if the whole draft is running unusually hot or cold on spending overall, that shows up as a small adjustment across the entire board, not just the position where it's happening. One honest limitation worth knowing: DEF and K aren't individually projected or valued in this tool; they're accounted for in your overall budget math (their roster spots reduce your discretionary budget the same as any other spot), but you won't see them priced as players on the board. Most leagues treat those as late $1 fills anyway, so this hasn't been a real gap in practice, but it's worth knowing going in. Track your own roster and remaining budget separately on the My Team tab. It's independent of the main board, just for your own team.
